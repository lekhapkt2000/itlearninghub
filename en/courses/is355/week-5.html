01 · PART A - DEFI OVERVIEW

What is DeFi?

DeFi - Decentralized Finance is an ecosystem of financial services implemented through Smart Contracts on the Blockchain, without necessarily requiring a direct intermediary organization for each step.
CriteriaTraditional financeDeFi
IntermediaryBank/financial institutionSmart Contract
AccountBank accountBlockchain Wallet
TransactionsThrough an intermediary systemOn-chain
TransparencyDepends on the institutionOn-chain data is auditable
RiskBank/Credit RiskSmart Contract/Oracle/Liquidity Risk
DeFi does not mean risk-free - It shifts part of the risk from a financial intermediary onto code, the market, and Blockchain mechanics.

5 main risk categories to analyze when designing any DeFi Lending product:

Risk typeCauseExample from this course
Smart Contract RiskLogic errors/vulnerabilities in the contract codeForgetting the Access Control assert in mint() (Week 3)
Liquidity RiskThe pool lacks enough liquidity to meet withdrawalsMany Borrowers draw loans at once, draining the Liquidity Pool
Default RiskA borrower fails to repayA Borrower defaults on a Green Lending loan
Liquidation RiskCollateral value drops sharplyCollateral Ratio falls below the Liquidation Threshold (section 05)
Oracle RiskOff-chain data (price, ESG Score) is delayed or manipulatedAn Oracle reports the wrong ESG Score, mispricing the interest rate (section 07)

02 · PART B - LIQUIDITY POOL

Liquidity Pool

Instead of transferring directly between two parties, DeFi can use a shared Pool: multiple Liquidity Providers (LP) deposit assets into the Pool, and borrowers can borrow from the Pool according to the Smart Contract's rules.

Alice, Bob, Carol (LP) ↓ Deposit Liquidity Pool ↓ Borrowers

Liquidity providers can earn Interest + Protocol Rewards, but bear Smart Contract/Market/Liquidity/Protocol risk. Borrowers get faster access to capital but must pay Interest, provide Collateral, and face the possibility of Liquidation.

03 · DEFI LENDING & P2P LENDING

DeFi Lending & P2P Lending

Peer-to-Peer Lending is a model where lenders and borrowers deal directly with each other through a Smart Contract:

Lender (Alice) ↓ Deposit Smart Contract ↓ Loan Borrower (Bob) ↓ Repayment + Interest Smart Contract ➜ Lender

04 · COLLATERAL

Collateral & the Collateral Ratio

What if the borrower doesn't repay? Many DeFi models use Overcollateralization to solve this problem.

Collateral Ratio = (Collateral Value / Loan Value) × 100%

Example: Collateral = $15,000, Loan = $10,000 ➜ Collateral Ratio = 150%.

05 · LIQUIDATION

Liquidation

If the collateral's price drops, the Collateral Ratio drops with it. When that ratio falls below a set level (the Liquidation Threshold), the Smart Contract can trigger Liquidation.

Check Collateral Ratio ↓ Ratio ≥ Threshold? Yes ➜ Continue / No ➜ Liquidation

Example: Collateral = $15,000, Loan = $10,000 ➜ Ratio = 150%. The Collateral price drops to $11,000 ➜ Ratio = 110%. If the Liquidation Threshold = 120%, the loan is liquidated because 110% < 120%.

06 · PART C - GREEN BANKING

Green Banking & ESG Score

In the context of this course, Green Banking is the application of financial technology to support environmental projects, renewable energy, clean production, and ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) investment.

CriteriaTraditional bankDeFi Green Lending Protocol
Credit verificationFinancial statements, manual reviewSmart Contract + Data Oracle (ESG Score)
CollateralReal estate, fixed assetsTokenized assets (Overcollateralization)
Interest pricingBank's fixed/floating rate scheduleDynamic Interest Rate based on ESG Score (ESG up ➜ Rate down)
Handling defaultLitigation, traditional debt collectionAutomatic On-chain liquidation when Collateral Ratio < Liquidation Threshold

Example ESG Score composed of 3 groups of metrics:

CriteriaScore
Environmental90
Social75
Governance80
Composite ESG Score82

07 · ESG-BASED LENDING & ORACLE

ESG-based Lending, automation & the Oracle problem

Idea: a company with a good ESG Score gets better loan terms. This is only a simulated rule for the exercise, not an actual credit-pricing framework:

ESG ScoreInterest Rate
90-1005%

70-797%

Where does the Smart Contract get the ESG Score from? The Blockchain does not inherently know the ESG Score - The data lives in an ESG Database, Company Report, IoT, Auditor, or External API, and must be brought in via an Oracle:

ESG Data (Off-chain) ↓ Oracle ↓ Smart Contract ↓ Loan Amount + Interest Rate

08 · PART D - SMART CONTRACT PRACTICE

The GreenLending.vy Smart Contract

A contract simulating a green lending system, managing borrowers, loans, collateral, ESG Score, interest rate, and loan status:

# @version ^0.4.3

"""
@title GreenLending
@notice Smart Contract mô phỏng cho vay xanh với lãi suất điều chỉnh theo ESG Score
"""

enum LoanStatus:
    ACTIVE
    PAID
    LIQUIDATED

event LoanCreated:
    loan_id: indexed(uint256)
    borrower: indexed(address)
    principal: uint256
    collateral: uint256
    esg_score: uint256
    interest_rate: uint256

event LoanRepaid:
    loan_id: indexed(uint256)
    total_paid: uint256

event LoanLiquidated:
    loan_id: indexed(uint256)
    collateral_ratio: uint256

event ESGUpdated:
    loan_id: indexed(uint256)
    old_score: uint256
    new_score: uint256

struct Loan:
    borrower: address
    principal: uint256
    collateral: uint256
    interest_rate: uint256
    esg_score: uint256
    start_time: uint256
    status: LoanStatus

LIQUIDATION_THRESHOLD: constant(uint256) = 120  # %

admin: public(address)
oracle: public(address)
loan_count: public(uint256)
loans: public(HashMap[uint256, Loan])

@deploy
def __init__():
    self.admin = msg.sender
    self.oracle = msg.sender
    self.loan_count = 0

@internal
@pure
def calculate_rate(esg_score: uint256) -> uint256:
    if esg_score >= 90:
        return 5  # 5% APY cho doanh nghiệp xanh xuất sắc
    elif esg_score >= 80:
        return 6  # 6% APY
    elif esg_score >= 70:
        return 7  # 7% APY
    return 9      # 9% APY phạt doanh nghiệp có chỉ số ESG thấp

@external
def createLoan(borrower: address, principal: uint256, collateral: uint256, esg_score: uint256) -> uint256:
    assert msg.sender == self.admin, "Only Admin can create loan"
    assert collateral * 100 / principal >= LIQUIDATION_THRESHOLD, "Collateral below threshold"

    rate: uint256 = self.calculate_rate(esg_score)
    loan_id: uint256 = self.loan_count

    self.loans[loan_id] = Loan(
        borrower=borrower,
        principal=principal,
        collateral=collateral,
        interest_rate=rate,
        esg_score=esg_score,
        start_time=block.timestamp,
        status=LoanStatus.ACTIVE
    )
    self.loan_count += 1

    log LoanCreated(loan_id, borrower, principal, collateral, esg_score, rate)
    return loan_id

@view
@external
def getCollateralRatio(loan_id: uint256) -> uint256:
    loan: Loan = self.loans[loan_id]
    return loan.collateral * 100 / loan.principal

@external
def updateESGScore(loan_id: uint256, new_score: uint256):
    assert msg.sender == self.oracle, "Only Oracle can update ESG score"
    loan: Loan = self.loans[loan_id]
    assert loan.status == LoanStatus.ACTIVE, "Loan is not active"

    old_score: uint256 = loan.esg_score
    self.loans[loan_id].esg_score = new_score
    self.loans[loan_id].interest_rate = self.calculate_rate(new_score)

    log ESGUpdated(loan_id, old_score, new_score)

@external
def repayLoan(loan_id: uint256):
    loan: Loan = self.loans[loan_id]
    assert loan.status == LoanStatus.ACTIVE, "Loan is not active"
    assert msg.sender == loan.borrower, "Only borrower can repay"

    elapsed: uint256 = block.timestamp - Loan.start_time
    interest: uint256 = loan.principal * loan.interest_rate * elapsed / (100 * 365 * 86400)
    total_paid: uint256 = loan.principal + interest

    self.loans[loan_id].status = LoanStatus.PAID
    log LoanRepaid(loan_id, total_paid)

@external
def liquidateLoan(loan_id: uint256, current_collateral_value: uint256):
    assert msg.sender == self.admin, "Only Admin can trigger liquidation"
    loan: Loan = self.loans[loan_id]
    assert loan.status == LoanStatus.ACTIVE, "Loan is not active"

    ratio: uint256 = current_collateral_value * 100 / loan.principal
    assert ratio < LIQUIDATION_THRESHOLD, "Collateral ratio still above threshold"

    self.loans[loan_id].status = LoanStatus.LIQUIDATED
    log LoanLiquidated(loan_id, ratio)
Note: This logic is a teaching illustration for a Smart Contract, not the interest calculation model or liquidation process of an actual bank/protocol.

09 · UNIT TESTING WITH PYTEST

A liquidateLoan & ESG Oracle test suite with pytest

Use the Ape Framework together with pytest to automatically test the contract's most important functions - Especially liquidateLoan() and updateESGScore(). Create the file tests/test_green_lending.py:

import pytest
from ape import project

@pytest.fixture
def admin(accounts):
    return accounts[0]

@pytest.fixture
def borrower(accounts):
    return accounts[1]

@pytest.fixture
def green_lending(admin, project):
    return admin.deploy(project.GreenLending)

def test_create_loan_sets_rate_from_esg_score(green_lending, admin, borrower):
    loan_id = green_lending.createLoan(borrower, 10_000, 15_000, 85, sender=admin)
    loan = green_lending.loans(loan_id)
    assert loan.interest_rate == 6

def test_oracle_updates_esg_score_and_recalculates_rate(green_lending, admin, borrower):
    loan_id = green_lending.createLoan(borrower, 10_000, 15_000, 85, sender=admin)

    # Oracle cập nhật ESG Score mới (tăng) cho khoản vay
    green_lending.updateESGScore(loan_id, 92, sender=admin)
    loan = green_lending.loans(loan_id)

    assert loan.esg_score == 92
    assert loan.interest_rate == 5  # Lãi suất giảm khi ESG tăng

def test_update_esg_score_rejects_non_oracle_caller(green_lending, admin, borrower):
    loan_id = green_lending.createLoan(borrower, 10_000, 15_000, 85, sender=admin)

    with pytest.raises(Exception):
        green_lending.updateESGScore(loan_id, 95, sender=borrower)

def test_liquidate_loan_when_ratio_drops_below_threshold(green_lending, admin, borrower):
    loan_id = green_lending.createLoan(borrower, 10_000, 15_000, 85, sender=admin)

    # Giá Collateral giảm xuống 11,000 -> Ratio = 110% < 120% threshold
    green_lending.liquidateLoan(loan_id, 11_000, sender=admin)
    loan = green_lending.loans(loan_id)

    assert loan.status == 2  # LIQUIDATED

def test_liquidate_loan_rejects_when_ratio_still_safe(green_lending, admin, borrower):
    loan_id = green_lending.createLoan(borrower, 10_000, 15_000, 85, sender=admin)

    # Collateral vẫn giữ 15,000 -> Ratio = 150% > 120% threshold, không đủ điều kiện thanh lý
    with pytest.raises(Exception):
        green_lending.liquidateLoan(loan_id, 15_000, sender=admin)

Run the whole test suite with:

ape test tests/test_green_lending.py -v
Students should add further test cases for repayLoan() and the case where createLoan() is rejected because the initial Collateral Ratio is already below the Liquidation Threshold.

11 · PART G - DATA & MACHINE LEARNING

Data & Machine Learning

This is an important part that connects Week 5 to Week 6 - AI & On-chain Analytics.

Default Risk Prediction

Use Random Forest or XGBoost trained on historical data (Interest Rate, Collateral Ratio, ESG Score, Income) to forecast the probability of default (Default Probability).

Historical Loan Data ↓ Feature Engineering ↓ Machine Learning ↓ Default Probability

Liquidation Risk Warning

Build a time-series model that forecasts collateral price movement to warn early about liquidation risk before the Collateral Ratio hits the Liquidation Threshold (e.g., 120%).

ESG Data Anomaly Detection

Apply Unsupervised Learning to check the ESG data for anomalies before an Oracle writes it to the Blockchain, reducing "Greenwashing" risk.

Green Investment Recommendation

A system that suggests a green investment portfolio based on an investor's Risk Tolerance and ESG Preference, recommending suitable Green Bond, Carbon Credit, Renewable Energy, or Green Token options.

14 · DISCUSSION QUESTIONS

Discussion questions

  1. Why does DeFi Lending usually require collateral?
  2. If the collateral's price drops sharply, how should the Smart Contract handle it?
  3. Should ESG Score be the sole factor determining the interest rate?
  4. Who is responsible if the ESG Score provided is wrong?
  5. How can the Oracle become a weak point in a DeFi system?
  6. Does Blockchain truly remove banks entirely from lending activity?
  7. How can Machine Learning help reduce Default and Liquidation risk?
  8. If an Oracle supplies a wrong ESG Score (due to an attack or off-chain data manipulation), what risk does the Smart Contract system bear, and how can this be mitigated with a Decentralized Oracle (e.g., Chainlink)?
  9. Overcollateralization fully solves anonymous credit risk, but why does it reduce Capital Efficiency for green businesses?
